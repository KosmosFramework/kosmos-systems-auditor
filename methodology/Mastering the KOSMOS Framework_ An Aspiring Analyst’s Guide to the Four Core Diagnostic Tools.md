#### 4\. Diagnostic Tool 3: The Designer Query Discriminator (DQD) – Uncovering Intent and Enforcement

Extractive systems often obscure their underlying mechanics to present designed exploitation as unalterable structural complexity. The  **Designer Query Discriminator (DQD)**  evaluates institutional intent, determining whether a system is designed to maintain systemic health or serve specific extractive interests.

##### The Three Quantitative Components of DQD

DQD evaluates institutional intent across three quantified metrics (each scored on a 0.00 to 1.00 scale):

1. **Designer Traceability (**  **\$DT**\$  **):**  Measures the proportion of active operational rules that are explicitly documented and traceable to specific human designers or legal architects.  \$\$\\text{DT} \= \\frac{\\text{Rules with Documented Designers}}{\\text{Total Active Operational Rules}}\$\$  
2. **Goal Alignment (**  **\$GA**\$  **):**  Quantifies the ratio of beneficial outcome efficiency relative to extractive asymmetry:  \$\$\\text{GA} \= 1 \- \\text{Extractive Ratio}\$\$  Where the Extractive Ratio represents the proportion of total system energy/capital diverted to non-functional administration, executive retention, or profit extraction.  
3. **Enforcement Dependency (**  **\$ED**\$  **):**  Quantifies the proportion of system processes that require active external force, legal coercion, or policing to persist rather than operating through autonomous cooperation:  \$\$\\text{ED} \= \\frac{\\text{Processes Requiring Coercive Enforcement}}{\\text{Total System Processes}}\$\$

##### The DQD Formula and Sample Calculation

\$\$\\text{DQD} \= \\frac{\\text{DT} \+ \\text{GA} \+ \\text{ED}}{3}\$\$

###### *Step-by-Step Mathematical Walkthrough*

Consider a diagnostic audit of an institutional healthcare provider:

1. **Designer Traceability (**  **\$DT**\$  **):**  Out of 500 audited operational and billing rules, 450 are directly traced to corporate governance and insurance policies.  \$\$\\text{DT} \= \\frac{450}{500} \= 0.90\$\$  
2. **Goal Alignment (**  **\$GA**\$  **):**  Financial analysis shows an Extractive Ratio of  \$0.58\$  ( \$58\\%\$  of expenditures go to administrative friction, shareholder dividends, and price markups rather than direct clinical care).  \$\$\\text{GA} \= 1 \- 0.58 \= 0.42\$\$  
3. **Enforcement Dependency (**  **\$ED**\$  **):**  Out of 300 core operating workflows, 240 rely on legal mandates, non-compete clauses, and mandatory coverage laws to maintain participation.  \$\$\\text{ED} \= \\frac{240}{300} \= 0.80\$\$  
4. **Baseline DQD Calculation:**   \$\$\\text{DQD}\_{\\text{baseline}} \= \\frac{0.90 \+ 0.42 \+ 0.80}{3} \= \\frac{2.12}{3} \\approx 0.707\$\$

#### 5\. Diagnostic Tool 4: The Observer Collapse Function (OCF) – Quantifying Fragility and Collapse Probability

The fourth tool in the KOSMOS pipeline is the  **Observer Collapse Function (OCF)** . OCF is a mathematical tool that measures a system's reliance on human belief and enforcement versus physical reality for its continued persistence. It quantifies systemic fragility and calculates collapse timelines under stress conditions.

##### The Core Variables of Baseline OCF

1. **Belief Recursion (**  **\$B\_R**\$  **):**  The proportion of system nodes or reinforcing circuits that depend on shared belief, narrative, or institutional perception rather than physical substrate reality ( \$0.00 \\text{ to } 1.00\$ ).  
2. **Observer Dependency (**  **\$D\_C**\$  **):**  The fraction of core operational processes that cease functioning if human observers withdraw their participation or belief ( \$0.00 \\text{ to } 1.00\$ ).  
3. **Intrinsic Stability (**  **\$T\_S**\$  **):**  The persistence ratio of the system's underlying design signal when active belief or enforcement is removed (measured as operational longevity with belief divided by longevity without belief).

\$\$\\text{OCF}\_{\\text{base}} \= \\frac{B\_R \\times D\_C}{T\_S}\$\$  
An  **\$\\text{OCF}\_{\\text{base}} \> 0.60**\$  indicates critical collapse risk.

##### The Complexity Acceleration Factor:  \$\\Psi(S)\$

Complex systems do not always degrade linearly; under specific structural conditions, complexity accelerates systemic fragility. When baseline OCF exceeds  \$0.60\$  in a system with low Distributed Agency ( \$DA\$ ), the analyst calculates the  **Complexity Adjustment**  **\$\\Psi(S)**\$ :  
\$\$\\Psi(S) \= S(C) \+ R(F) \+ C(N)\$\$

* **Control Stability**  **\$S(C)**\$  **:**  Measures centralization of enforcement points ( \$0.00 \\text{ to } 1.00\$ ). High control centralization creates single points of failure.  
* **Feedback Responsiveness**  **\$R(F)**\$  **:**  Measures the corruption or failure of error-correction signals ( \$0.00 \\text{ to } 1.00\$ ). Unresponsive feedback causes systemic errors to compound.  
* **Interface Connectivity**  **\$C(N)**\$  **:**  Measures tight coupling across system interfaces without operational redundancy ( \$0.00 \\text{ to } 1.00\$ ), causing localized failures to cascade across the network.

\$\$\\text{OCF}*{\\text{adj}} \= \\text{OCF}*{\\text{base}} \\times \\left1 \+ \\Psi(S) \\times \\left(1 \- \\frac{\\text{DA}}{10}\\right)\\right\$\$

Using empirical data from the US Healthcare System audit:
